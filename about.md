# {{ page.title }}

These pages are the product of an ongoing, multi-year collaboration between the American Philosophical Society's [Center for Native American and Indigenous Research](https://www.amphilsoc.org/library/CNAIR) (CNAIR) and the Tunica-Biloxi Tribe of Louisiana's [Language and Cultural Revitalization Program](https://www.tunicabiloxi.org/tribal-info/departments/language-culture-revitalization-program/) (LCRP). The core components of this project were part of a grant project from 2020 to 2024 funded by the National Science Foundation’s Dynamic Language Infrastructure program.

CNAIR is deeply grateful to the Tunica-Biloxi Tribe of Louisiana and its Language & Cultural Revitalization Program for their generosity and in providing their time and expertise on this project, and for the trust they have given in supporting this collaboration. 

CNAIR first met members of the Tunica-Biloxi Tribe through Councilwoman Brenda Lintinger, who visited the APS Library in 2013 and introduced CNAIR to the LCRP. This project was first conceived of in 2017 the Tunica-Biloxi LCRP, CNAIR, and the lead linguist on the project, Dr. Patricia Anderson, a long-time participant in the Tunica Language Working Group (or Kuhpani Yoyani Luhchi Yoroni – KYLY).

The project has two main components. The first component is a full transcription of the 14 notebooks recorded by the linguist Mary Haas and Sesostrie Youchigant, the last known fluent speaker of Tunica. The text of the originals is then transcribed into the contemporary Tunica writing system. 

These notebooks, housed in the archives of the APS Library & Museum, were digitized by CNAIR. Starting in the autumn of 2020, they have been gradually transcribed and transliterated by numerous people, primarily Tunica-Biloxi tribal members, with additional assistance by linguists associated with KYLY and by staff at CNAIR. 

CNAIR is currently exploring ways to integrate the transcriptions of the original materials into aiding the navigation of the digitized originals.

The second major component of the project is the creation of an overall portal to the Tunica archival resources at the APS Library & Museum. This portal provides: 

* Multiple ways to [browse](/tunicalanguageresources/notebooks) the stories, such as by topic, title, or sequence in the notebooks  
* [Video commentaries and reflections](/tunicalanguageresources/tunica-voices/) by Tunica community members on Tunica language, culture, and history  
* Short [articles](/tunicalanguageresources/additional-resources/insights/) giving linguistic context  
* Additional Tunica language [resources](/tunicalanguageresources/additional-resources/)  
* Digitized archival [photographs](/tunicalanguageresources/additional-resources/photographs/) of the Tunica community from the APS collections


The current website is a starting point for further collaboration, providing a space to create and add more content to further illuminate these materials, especially through the perspectives of Tunica-Biloxi community members themselves.
